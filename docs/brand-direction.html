02 / The ideaPositioning

Front Line

Their own sentence — "Putting Quality in FRONT LINE" — is better than anything a studio would write for them. The front line is the theatre, the ICU, the NICU, the lab bench at 3am. STEM MEDICA equips it and then stands on it. Everything below is derived from that one phrase plus the stem-cell root in the name: precision instruments, and things that grow back.

  • The visual world is the device, not the doctorNo stock photography of smiling clinicians — every competitor in Addis has that, and it reads as a template. The material is device fascia: calibration ticks, serial plates, ECG baselines, CE and ISO marks, spec tables in monospace. Photography is their own installation shots, shot straight, captioned like a service record.
  • Swiss precision is earned, not borrowedThey distribute SCHILLER — actual Swiss-engineered instruments. A strict International-Typographic grid is a legitimate reference here, not a mood board pull.
  • The logo sets the palette, not a mood boardBlue and red are already theirs and already on every quotation and business card. The job isn't to invent a colour story — it's to give those two colours strict roles and hold the line on them.
  • Proof over adjectives"Advanced", "innovative", "world-class" are free — anyone can type them. Serial numbers, install dates, hospital departments, training-day counts and brand certificates are not.
03 / PaletteSampled from the logo

Their blue, their red, and nothing else that shouts

The logo already fixes this: a saturated royal blue for STEM, a scarlet for MEDICA. Blue-and-red is the most conventional pairing in medicine, so the differentiation can't come from the hues — it comes from discipline. Blue carries the structure. Red does exactly one job: attention. It is the call button, the active state, the ECG trace, the urgent tender — and nothing else on the page is allowed to be loud enough to compete with it.

#1A3E8F
Navy — primary
Sampled from the STEM wordmark. Links, active nav, headings on paper, chart series. The structural colour.
#0F2555
Navy Deep — grounds
Masthead, hero, footer. A darkened extension of the logo blue so dark sections stay in the family instead of going generic black.
#E12A22
Scarlet — the signal
The MEDICA red. Call button, ECG trace, alerts, one key figure. Never a section fill, never a decorative bar.
#111A2E
Ink — type & rules
Body copy and the 2px module rules. Near-black pulled toward the logo blue, so it reads as part of the palette rather than sitting on top of it.
#EFF1F4
Paper — ground
Default page. Cool rather than cream, tinted a few degrees toward the blue. Pure #FFF is reserved for panels that need to lift off it.
#6B7385
Steel — neutral
Labels, captions, disabled states, table rules. Blue-biased grey — a neutral that was chosen, not inherited.
  • Ratio, roughly 70 / 25 / 5Paper and ink do most of the work, navy carries structure and dark sections, red gets about five percent of any given screen. The moment red goes above that it stops meaning anything.
  • Contrast is checked, not assumedNavy #1A3E8F on paper clears AA comfortably. Scarlet #E12A22 does not pass for small body text on light — so red text uses the darkened #B31E18, and the bright scarlet is kept for fills, strokes and large type.
  • Dark theme lifts the blue, not the redOn the dark ground the navy brightens to #3B6BD4 to stay legible; the red moves only slightly to #F2564C. The relationship between them stays the same in both themes.
04 / TypographyArchivo · IBM Plex Mono · Noto Ethiopic

One grotesque, stretched and squeezed on purpose

Archivo is a variable family with a real width axis, so the whole site runs on one typeface that behaves like three: expanded and uppercase for display, normal for text, condensed for dense navigation. That's a single font file doing the work of a pairing — fast on Ethiopian mobile data, and it is not Inter or Space Grotesk. IBM Plex Mono handles every number, model code and label, which is what instrument panels actually look like.

05 / The markWorking with what exists

Keep the equity, fix the small sizes

The existing logo is a two-part lockup: an oblique bold STEM in blue with MEDICA set smaller in red beneath it, plus a circular emblem of red discs and blue figures orbiting a centre — people around a cell, which is exactly right for a name taken from stem-cell generation. The idea is sound. The problem is purely mechanical: that emblem has four or five distinct shapes in it, and below about 40px they merge into a blob. A favicon, a WhatsApp avatar and an app icon are all smaller than that.

Now — approximate

Five shapes, two reds at different scales, thin negative space between the blue forms. Detail-rich at print size, mush at 32px.

Reduction — recommended

The same gesture in two shapes: a broken blue orbit around a solid red centre. Survives 16px, one-colour print, embroidery and a laser-etched asset tag.

Secondary device

A single ECG complex in scarlet. Not a logo — a system element for dividers, loading states and section rules. Already used across this page.

  • Two lockups, not oneHorizontal (emblem left, STEM MEDICA stacked right) for the site header and letterhead; the emblem alone for favicon, WhatsApp Business, social avatars and equipment asset tags. Define the clear space as the radius of the red centre on all four sides.
  • Leave the oblique alone — and don't echo itThe slant in STEM is the logo's own signature. The page around it should stay upright; if the headlines lean too, the whole thing reads as a 1990s sports brand. One italic on the page, and the logo owns it.
  • Make the red hierarchy deliberateRight now MEDICA is red and smaller, which reads as the subordinate half. That's fine — but it means red is already doing "accent" in the logo, which is exactly the role it plays on the site. Consistent, and free.
  • What I'd want before touching the artworkThe vector original. Everything above is drawn from a small raster, so the curves are approximations, and the exact brand hexes should be pulled from the source file rather than eyedropped from a JPEG.
06 / Mobile firstLayout system

An instrument readout, one column deep

Every section is a module with a 2px rule across the top carrying a monospace label on the left and a count or status on the right — the way a device reports itself. On a phone the modules stack; on desktop the same rules become a 12-column grid with a persistent contact rail. Nothing is a rounded card: border, fill and shadow get spent only where something genuinely needs lifting.

  • The phone number is the whole funnelA fixed two-button bar sits on the thumb from the first scroll: tel: on the left in scarlet, WhatsApp on the right in ink. Procurement staff in Addis call — they don't fill in contact forms.
  • Browse by department, not by brandA hospital buyer thinks "we need something for the NICU", not "we need AMBULANC". Brands live on a page of their own, as credentials.
  • Installation log as a live sectionThe recent-installations feed is the proof engine, and it's already being written — every LinkedIn post is one entry. Date, device, department, whether training was delivered.
  • Spec tables scroll, they never squeezeMonospace spec blocks get their own horizontal scroll container so an 8-column table never shoves the page sideways on a 360px screen.
  • Built for a slow connectionOne variable font file, SVG marks, no icon library, no carousel, images lazily loaded and served as WebP. Target under 150KB before photography — that is a design decision on Ethiopian mobile, not a technical footnote.
  • 48px targets, visible focus ringsA scarlet focus outline on light and on the navy ground — never the browser default stripped without a replacement.
